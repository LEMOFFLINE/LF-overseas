# SEO rollout

## Implemented on-page work

- Homepage title is `Custom Apparel & Uniform Manufacturer in China | LF Clothing`. The user confirmed on 18 November 2026 that LF owns its garment factory. Homepage, About, product, customization and quality copy identify LF as a manufacturer with its own factory; complementary accessories remain part of the same-order offering.
- Homepage copy highlights reference-image development without a complete tech pack, regular clothing usually starting at 50 pieces per style/colour, and clothing plus accessories coordinated in one order. Material and customization requirements can affect minimums.
- Separate homepage links direct brand buyers to garment development and business buyers to coordinated staff uniforms. Existing product category URLs remain unchanged.
- Current content work focuses on custom clothing development: reference images, requirements, samples and order confirmation. Product-specific guide drafts have been withdrawn; the development route, price and schedule are reviewed for each project.
- `/guides/` contains one general buyer article about starting custom clothing from a reference image. It covers requirements and sample decisions, with links to development services and inquiries.
- The guide hub and general article are available in all eight languages, included in the sitemap and language routing, and linked from the main navigation, homepage, customization page and relevant reference pages. Article structured data identifies LF as the author without invented dates or reviews.
- Customization and guide FAQs explain that sampling is quoted after requirement review, usually at around twice the bulk unit price. Sample fees can be credited against bulk orders of at least 200 pieces of the same style. Sample shipping is charged separately, with samples usually sent by express courier on DDP terms.
- User-confirmed customization options now include printing, embroidery, wash effects, labels and custom trims. Suitable combinations and final appearance are confirmed through sampling. Reasonable sample adjustments are included; bulk production follows the approved sample and specifications.
- Fabric selection starts with intended use and requirements for water resistance, breathability, texture and colour. LF proposes the fabric and matching processes for buyer confirmation before sampling; no physical swatch promise is added.
- Regular bulk orders up to 500 pieces usually take 2-3 weeks in production, counted from receipt of a 30% deposit. Shipping time is additional. This shared policy appears in customization, the guide, buyer-program FAQs and the inquiry page; the remaining 70% balance is paid in full before shipment. Video or in-person inspection is available before dispatch.
- The customization page opens with shared order notes for MOQ, sampling, production, payment and inspection. The inquiry sidebar uses the same notes, and dispatch information is aligned on the process page.
- LF records inspection and packing videos before shipment. Garments with obvious issues found during inspection are remade before packing. Post-delivery defects are reviewed against the approved sample and specifications, with compensation agreed case by case based on defect rate and other relevant factors. No fixed compensation amount, claim deadline or zero-defect guarantee is stated.
- Existing design-reference URLs remain available; their calls to action ask buyers to discuss a design. They do not establish that each illustrated design is a mature, production-ready model.
- Homepage proof links cover the 10-suit specialist project, 500 ANA ground-crew coveralls and 2,000 Mercedes-Benz winter event outfits. The Mercedes-Benz project delivery is 28 days; completed-project timings are not general lead-time promises.
- Each of the five product collections has a unique supplier-led title and buyer-focused meta description.
- The core positioning covers custom workwear, uniforms and branded apparel for B2B buyers, with accessories as complementary products in the same order.
- The School, Team & Staff collection targets school-uniform and education-program demand using the existing tracksuit, polo, hoodie, softshell and tailored-product directions.
- `/collections/aviation-flight-suits/` remains a secondary page documenting specialist development. It is linked from the homepage project proof, product navigation and relevant industrial content.
- The dedicated 2019 winter-event case covers 2,000 four-piece outfits. The aviation page documents the 10-suit development project, and the project overview includes ANA and Apple garment references. No new client outcomes or certification claims have been invented.

## External link acquisition

External backlinks cannot be placed from the website repository. They require access to a customer, partner, directory, association, publication or social account. Use this order of effort:

1. Ask completed-project customers and procurement partners to link to the most relevant LF collection or project page from a supplier, partner or case-study page.
2. Complete legitimate company profiles on industry associations, chambers of commerce and B2B directories using the exact company name, address, email and homepage URL.
3. Publish a short, evidence-backed project summary on the company's LinkedIn and Facebook profiles, linking to `/projects/` rather than repeating generic homepage links.
4. Offer useful specification-led contributions to workwear, PPE, uniform and aviation-supply publications. Link only where the article genuinely supports the reader.
5. Avoid paid link networks, bulk directory submissions, reciprocal-link pages and keyword-stuffed guest posts.

Recommended link targets:

- Homepage for general company profiles.
- `/collections/industrial-workwear/` for workwear supplier references.
- `/collections/protective-hi-vis/` for hi-vis, FR-option and anti-static topics.
- `/collections/team-staff-apparel/` for school-uniform, education and teamwear topics.
- `/collections/aviation-flight-suits/` only for aviation-specific topics.
- `/projects/` when discussing delivery coordination or the documented outerwear example.

## Search Console measurement baseline

Record the deployment date before requesting re-indexing. Compare equivalent 28-day periods rather than day-to-day movement.

Track these page groups separately:

- Homepage
- Five product collections
- Aviation flight-suit landing page
- Product detail pages
- Project example
- Customization guide hub and individual articles

Track these metrics:

- Indexed status and last crawl date
- Impressions, clicks, click-through rate and average position
- Queries containing `workwear`, `uniform`, `coverall`, `hi vis`, `anti static`, `flight suit` and `aviation`
- Countries generating impressions and qualified inquiries
- Pages with impressions but low click-through rate, which are candidates for title and description testing

Suggested checkpoints:

- Day 0: deploy, submit the sitemap and request indexing for the homepage, four priority product pages, supporting collections, projects page and aviation page.
- Day 7: confirm crawling and index status; do not judge ranking performance yet.
- Day 28: compare the first full 28-day period with the previous 28 days.
- Day 56: keep titles stable unless the data shows a clear mismatch between queries, impressions and click-through rate.

Do not continue changing the homepage during the initial measurement window unless there is a factual, legal or technical error.
